Every night at exactly 11:11, a blue door appeared at the end of Maple Street.

Nobody knew where it came from.

It had no house around it, no fence, and no path leading to it. Just a door standing upright in the grass.

One night, twelve-year-old Milo decided to open it.

Behind the door was his bedroom.

Not his bedroom as it was now, but his bedroom ten years in the future. The walls were covered with photographs, the desk was piled with books, and an old version of Milo sat by the window.

Future Milo looked up.

“You’re early,” he said.

“Early for what?”

Future Milo smiled sadly.

“For forgetting.”

Then the door slammed shut.

The next morning, the blue door was gone.

Milo spent years wondering what he was supposed to forget.

He searched through old photographs, journals, and childhood memories, convinced that somewhere inside them was the answer. But nothing seemed unusual.

Until his seventeenth birthday.

While cleaning out the attic, Milo discovered a dusty wooden box with his name written on it. Inside was a stack of photographs.

The first showed Milo at seven years old.

The second showed him at twelve, standing at the end of Maple Street.

The third made his stomach drop.

It showed Future Milo sitting beside the blue door.

Milo stared at the photograph.

Then he noticed something he had never seen before.

In the corner of the picture stood a little girl.

She was holding Milo's hand.

He didn't recognize her.

On the back of the photograph were the words:

Her name is Lily. You promised you would remember her.

Milo froze.

He had no sister.

No childhood friend named Lily.

As he searched his memories, a sharp pain suddenly flashed through his head.

For a moment, he remembered a little girl laughing beside him.

Then the memory vanished.

And Milo realized the terrifying truth.

The blue door wasn't showing him the future.

It was showing him the memories he had already lost.

That night, at exactly 11:11, the blue door appeared again.

Milo ran toward it.

This time, he didn't hesitate.

He opened the door.

Behind it was the same bedroom, ten years in the future.

But Future Milo wasn't sitting by the window anymore.

He was standing directly in front of the door.

And beside him was Lily.

She looked exactly as she had in the photograph.

Future Milo's face went pale.

“You weren't supposed to remember her yet.”

Milo stared at the girl.

“Who is she?”

Lily smiled.

Then she whispered,

“The person you forgot saving.”

Suddenly, every light in the room went out.

When they came back on, Lily was gone.

Future Milo was gone too.

Only a photograph remained on the floor.

Milo picked it up.

It showed him standing beside Lily.

Behind them was the blue door.

And written across the bottom, in red ink, were six words:

You opened it ten years too early.

The door slammed shut.

The next morning, the blue door was gone.

Milo spent the next five years trying to find Lily.

Then, on his twenty-second birthday, he found an old photograph in a box beneath his bed.

It showed him standing beside a blue door.

On the back, in his own handwriting, were four words:

Don't open it again.

Then the door opened by itself and the words "I see you" came ringing out.

Then everyone died and lived a happy life.
