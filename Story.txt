Every night at exactly 11:11, a blue door appeared at the end of Maple Street.

Nobody knew where it came from.

It had no house around it, no fence, and no path leading to it. Just a door standing upright in the grass.

One night, twelve-year-old Milo decided to open it.

Behind the door was his bedroom.

Not his bedroom as it was now, but his bedroom ten years in the future. The walls were covered with photographs, the desk was piled with books, and an old version of Milo sat by the window.

Future Milo looked up.

“You’re early,” he said.

“Early for what?”

Future Milo smiled sadly.

“For forgetting.”

Then the door slammed shut.

The next morning, the blue door was gone.

Milo spent years wondering what he was supposed to forget.

Then, on his twenty-second birthday, he found an old photograph in a box beneath his bed.

It showed him standing beside a blue door.

On the back, in his own handwriting, were four words:

Don't open it again.
